The Quiet Engineering of Tide Clocks

By M. Harlow · 6 min read

Long before satellites, harbour masters relied on tide clocks whose dials turned once every lunar day.

A dial that runs slow on purpose

A tide clock completes its cycle every 12 hours and 25 minutes, matching the moon rather than the sun.

Set it at high tide, and it will stay honest for months.

Local geography still matters: estuaries and bays can shift the timing by an hour or more.
